# Research follow-up: stop broad reflow and measure the actual bottleneck

Research date: 2026-10-13. Phase 5 remains incomplete. This report proposes work; it does not change frozen hardware, open the held-out set, tick phase gates, or claim a new routing pass.

The current decision is concrete: retain the fanout-clean 133-overflow control. Paired native route [38453870785](https://github.com/Kanishk234/protocol-emulator-asic/actions/runs/38453870785) reports 6000 overflow for the native-feedback placement, versus 133 for the control. The earlier RUDY placement had 6544 in [38321208948](https://github.com/Kanishk234/protocol-emulator-asic/actions/runs/38321208948). Changing the estimator did not rescue broad redistribution. The new candidate's Metal1 resource count also drops from 16596 to 13283, while Metal1 overflow rises from 13 to 3442 and Metal2 from 53 to 1795. This motivates examining actual available edges and blockage attribution; it does not establish why those resources changed.

## What the primary sources actually support

- [OpenROAD global placement](https://openroad.readthedocs.io/en/latest/main/src/gpl/README.html) documents RUDY as the default and native FastRoute feedback as more precise but more expensive. Neither method guarantees closure. WARP has now measured failure with both; repeating broad reflow without another causal hypothesis is not justified.
- [OpenROAD global routing](https://openroad.readthedocs.io/en/latest/main/src/grt/README.html) provides congestion iteration reporting, net route visualization and layer-wise wirelength reporting. These support a net/edge audit. Current documentation includes features newer than the pinned image: inspect the installed command/API and pin source hashes before adding a collector.
- [OpenROAD detailed placement](https://openroad.readthedocs.io/en/latest/main/src/dpl/README.html) supports per-instance padding and fragmented rows; mirroring targets wirelength. These are tools, not evidence of a WARP gain. Our broad padding failed legalization and mirroring/local probes regressed, so do not repeat them. Actual ROW power phase remains independently checked rather than inferred from row index.
- [PRISM's competition repository](https://github.com/kdp1965/ihp-um-janestreet-prism) explicitly documents configuration macro columns acting as routing walls and restricts what may sit across them. The transferable lesson is to measure usable routing topology and pin-facing placement. Its macro geometry and layout are different; its result does not prove a WARP cut is saturated. Its compiler and [Tiny Tapeout documentation](https://tinytapeout.com/chips/ttihp26b/tt_um_pettit_prism_lite) also show the value of a complete compiler/load/debug workflow. Improving WARP's fit diagnostics and reproducible examples is useful without adopting its architecture.
- [Tiny FABulous SKY26a](https://github.com/mole99/tt-fabulous-sky-26a) explicitly separates preinitialized emulation, real RTL loading and gate-level simulation, and stitches previously implemented tile views. WARP should preserve that distinction in its evidence and finish native tile/interface coverage. Its SKY130 results are not an equal-area CMOS5L comparison.
- [FABulous upstream](https://github.com/FPGA-Research/FABulous) warns that development versions may break compatibility and recommends matched release documentation. An upstream update is a separately controlled tool experiment, not a blind routing fix.
- The authors' [How to Shrink My FPGAs](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/207833524/FPGA_2020_FABulous_optimizations_1_.pdf) investigates fabric implementation optimizations including configuration mapping and wire permutation. That supports a future generated-interface experiment with matching compiler changes, not hand-editing today's generated fabric or assuming the published gains transfer.

## Successor ideas requiring a separate decision and qualification

These remain research proposals, not work to silently merge into frozen G1. They are ranked below by relevance to the current evidence. Any contract change needs approval; an architectural successor needs its own hardware freeze and a newly sealed evaluation set because previously evaluated protocols cannot honestly serve as unseen tests again. Preserve G1 and its existing results. Physical-only experiments retain the original contract and still require matched requalification:

1. **Macro pin and configuration-wire ordering.** If the cut/net audit identifies crossings imposed by pin ordering, regenerate tile interfaces and configuration mapping together. Verify compiler/bitstream round trips, every affected interface, loaded behavior and equal-total-area physical results. Physical pin changes may preserve the logical contract but still require a documented successor decision.
2. **Shell/fabric floorplan repartition.** If usable corridor capacity is the limit, allocate shell logic and macro pin faces together, or test a different legal macro position. Compare one geometry policy at a time under the same tile envelope and timing target. More whitespace in the wrong corridor is not a solution.
3. **Configuration storage/enable topology.** Existing delay-cell observations justify investigating the cost, not deleting hold repairs. Any new storage or clock-enable scheme needs supported cells, reset/configuration semantics, equivalence, clock checks and the complete loading flow. Pinned IHP clock-gate cells marked `dont_use` are not a shortcut.
4. **Memory or deeper queues.** Add only if measured host-service and burst limits establish a benefit across multiple design-set workloads. Macro area, power access, pin walls, compiler mapping and initialization/reset all count. SRAM is not automatically the answer to routing congestion.
5. **Primitive/local-route refinement.** Evaluate shared generic timer/shift/CRC/event resources from measured design-set bottlenecks, with usable mapping and routing support. No dedicated protocol controller, no held-out tuning, no area-only winner.

The immediate competition gain is a reliable, explainable entry: complete matched evidence and usable tools on G1 while compact work follows measured routing topology. None of these proposals establishes that WARP is universally programmable, faster than a CPU/PIO design, or ready for silicon signoff.
